Apoideia is a memory and learning runtime for AI agents. There are two ways to use it and these terms cover both: running the software yourself, and signing into a gateway someone operates.

The software is Apache-2.0

The gateway, the console, this site and both SDKs are licensed under the Apache License, Version 2.0 — the memory engine included. The retrieval engine is MIT. Those licences govern your use of the code, including the warranty disclaimer and limitation of liability in them — nothing on this page narrows the rights they grant you.

If you self-host, that licence is the whole relationship. You need no account with anyone, and no part of your deployment reports to us.

Accounts

An account on a gateway deployment is an email address and a password. In v0.1 an account belongs to exactly one organisation, and there are no invites, roles or seats.

  • You are responsible for what is done with your credentials and with any API key minted from your account. Keys are shown once and stored only as a salted hash, so a key you lose is revoked and replaced rather than recovered.
  • Do not use an account to attack the deployment it belongs to, to work around its quotas, or to store data you have no right to store.
  • Whoever operates the deployment may suspend an account that does one of those things. Where you self-host, that person is you.

Paying, if you ever do

There is a free plan and it stays free. If you self-host, that is the only plan there is: the software has no billing in it unless whoever runs the deployment configures a payment provider, and yours will not have.

On a deployment that does sell plans, three things are true. Payment is handled by Stripe and no card details reach this software at any point — you are redirected to Stripe to pay and to Stripe's billing portal to change a card, change a plan or cancel. Usage past a paid plan's included allowance is metered from the counters the product already keeps, which you can read yourself at any time under Usage. And an existing account is never silently converted into a paid one: an upgrade is something you do, not something that happens.

If a subscription lapses — a card expires, a payment fails past the point of retrying — the account is not cut off. It returns to the free plan's limits, keeps its data, and keeps answering. What it loses is headroom.

What is not promised

  • No uptime commitment. v0.1 is a single gateway process, a single Postgres and a single memory-engine instance, as Tradeoffs says plainly. There is no SLA.
  • The measurements are measurements, not predictions. Every figure on the performance page is rendered from a run's own output file and each is reproducible from this repository. None of them is a promise about your workload.
  • Data durability is the operator's, not ours. Backups of a deployment's Postgres and object store belong to whoever runs it.

Changes

These terms will change as the product does — v0.1 is an early release and says so everywhere. Material changes will be dated at the top of this page rather than applied quietly.
